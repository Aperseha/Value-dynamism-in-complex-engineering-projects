# Value Dynamism in Complex Engineering Projects

### Supplementary Materials — Systematic Literature Review

**Article:** *Value Dynamism in Complex Engineering Projects: A Systematic Literature Review of Methodological Artefacts for Managing Semantic, Decisional, and Political Closures*

**Authors:** Antoine Persehais, Thierry Gidel, Pierre Emmanuel Fayemi

**Affiliations:** Université de Technologie de Compiègne (UTC) — IKOS Lab

---

## 1. Overview

This repository provides supplementary materials supporting a Systematic Literature Review (SLR) investigating how methodological artefacts — including methods, models, and tools — address the dynamic evolution of stakeholder values throughout complex engineering projects.

The review examines a fundamental challenge in value-driven design: **stakeholder values evolve throughout the project lifecycle, while engineering decisions, technical specifications, and governance arrangements tend to become progressively stabilised.**

This misalignment may produce a *Compliance-Value Gap*, in which a project successfully satisfies its initial specifications but no longer adequately addresses the evolving expectations of its stakeholders.

To investigate this challenge, the review introduces a **Triple Closure Framework**, distinguishing three interrelated mechanisms through which value evolution may become constrained:

* **Semantic Closure:** Difficulties in expressing, interpreting, translating, or revising stakeholder values across heterogeneous actors and disciplines.
* **Decisional Closure:** Premature technical or organisational commitments that restrict the reconsideration of design alternatives.
* **Political Closure:** Governance structures, institutional arrangements, or power relationships that prevent the legitimate renegotiation of project priorities and purposes.

The review identifies **24 relevant scientific contributions** and classifies their methodological approaches into **10 strategies (A–J)** addressing these three dimensions.

## 3. Taxonomy of Identified Strategies

The SLR identifies ten methodological strategies organised into three response clusters.

### 3.1 Semantic Closure — Axiological Grounding

This cluster concerns the representation, interpretation, translation, and formalisation of stakeholder values.

| Strategy | Name                                | Principal Mechanism                                                                     |
| -------- | ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A        | Value dependency unveiling          | Mapping value exchanges, relationships, and dependencies                                |
| B        | Active value interfacing            | Mediating between heterogeneous stakeholder and engineering perspectives                |
| C        | Multi-layer design and sense-making | Connecting technical characteristics, stakeholder expectations, and higher-level values |
| D        | Parametric value engineering        | Translating stakeholder values into measurable engineering parameters                   |
| E        | Computer-based value alignment      | Using computational approaches to explore complex value-related design spaces           |
| F        | Mathematical value harmonisation    | Formalising stakeholder consensus, uncertainty, and value conflicts                     |

Together, these strategies provide mechanisms for establishing a common representation of value and connecting stakeholder expectations to engineering activities.

Their principal limitation concerns the continuous maintenance of these representations as stakeholder priorities, technical configurations, and project contexts evolve.

### 3.2 Decisional Closure — Design Space Liquidity

This cluster addresses premature commitment and the loss of flexibility within engineering processes.

| Strategy | Name                             | Principal Mechanism                                                                   |
| -------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| G        | Option-based value-driven design | Maintaining multiple design alternatives and delaying irreversible commitments        |
| H        | Flexible stage-gate models       | Enabling iterative learning and the controlled reopening of previous design decisions |

These strategies contribute to **Design Space Liquidity**, understood as the capacity to preserve meaningful design alternatives and reconsider commitments when new information emerges.

However, technical flexibility does not necessarily imply that an organisation has the authority or willingness to reconsider established decisions.

### 3.3 Political Closure — Strategic and Temporal Resilience

This cluster concerns the legitimacy of change, evolving stakeholder relationships, and the capacity of governance structures to accommodate revised priorities.

| Strategy | Name                                       | Principal Mechanism                                                                          |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| I        | Foresight-driven value architecture        | Anticipating contextual changes and establishing a rationale for future strategic adaptation |
| J        | Dynamic stakeholder negotiation frameworks | Supporting continuous renegotiation of stakeholder roles, influence, and value priorities    |

These strategies seek to preserve the project's capacity to adapt when stakeholder configurations, institutional conditions, or strategic objectives change.

The reviewed literature nevertheless reveals limited operational support for lightweight and continuous political re-arbitration.

## 4. Classification Approach

The classification follows the analytical framework described in the accompanying article.

Each reviewed contribution was examined according to two complementary perspectives:

**1. Methodological mechanism**

Identification of the operating principles through which the proposed artefact represents, translates, evaluates, preserves, or renegotiates stakeholder value.

**2. Closure mitigation**

Assessment of how these mechanisms contribute to addressing Semantic, Decisional, or Political Closure.

This approach distinguishes the intended objectives of methodological artefacts from the mechanisms through which they are expected to operate.

### Interpretation of the Justifications

The justification columns explain the reasoning used to associate each publication with one or several strategies.

They describe the relevant methodological mechanism and its potential contribution to mitigating a particular closure.

These justifications should be understood as **analytical interpretations developed within the SLR**, rather than independent experimental demonstrations that each method effectively resolves the corresponding closure.

Likewise, an empty classification indicates that no contribution was assigned to that dimension within the review. It does not establish that the publication has no possible relevance to that dimension.

## 5. Main Research Findings

The cross-analysis highlights three principal findings.

**Semantic mechanisms are comparatively mature.** The literature provides a broad range of approaches for representing, structuring, quantifying, and communicating stakeholder values. However, maintaining these representations and their traceability over time remains challenging.

**Decisional mechanisms offer substantial technical flexibility.** Set-based design, scenario exploration, and iterative development models make it possible to preserve alternatives and reconsider previous choices. Nevertheless, the ability to modify a technical architecture does not automatically guarantee organisational authorisation for change.

**Political adaptability remains less operationalised.** While foresight and stakeholder negotiation approaches provide important conceptual foundations, their implementation often depends on extensive scenario modelling, expert facilitation, or governance arrangements that are difficult to formalise.

These findings suggest that the primary research challenge is not necessarily the absence of individual methodological mechanisms, but rather their **integration into a coherent, lightweight, and temporally adaptable approach**.

Importantly, the three closures are not sequential stages. They interact throughout the lifecycle, and an artefact addressing one dimension may simultaneously influence the others.

## 6. Relationship to the Accompanying Article

The accompanying scientific article presents:

* The theoretical foundations of Value Dynamism and the Triple Closure Framework.
* The systematic literature review protocol and study selection process.
* The taxonomy of ten methodological strategies.
* A cross-criteria analysis of the operational contributions, maturity, and limitations of these strategies.
* A synthesis of complementarities, dependencies, and remaining research gaps.

To preserve the readability of the article, the publication focuses on the synthesis and interpretation of the strategies rather than reproducing every article-level classification justification.

**This repository provides the detailed supporting evidence for those classifications.**

It allows readers to trace the connections between individual publications, methodological strategies, and closure dimensions, complementing the higher-level discussion presented in the article.

The repository supports transparency and inspection of the analytical coding. It does not, by itself, reproduce the complete literature search and selection procedure.

## 7. Research Perspectives

The findings provide a conceptual foundation for future research into integrated value-management methodologies capable of combining:

* Continuous traceability between stakeholders, value concepts, value dimensions, engineering characteristics, and design decisions.
* Temporal representation of changing stakeholder expectations and project contexts.
* Preservation of alternative technical configurations and controlled reopening of design commitments.
* Reassessment of stakeholder influence, priorities, and governance arrangements.
* Sufficient methodological simplicity for repeated application in industrial projects.

These directions inform the development of the **Value Co-evolution and Liquidity Framework (VCLF)**, a proposed integrating framework intended to connect semantic alignment, decisional flexibility, and political adaptability throughout the project lifecycle.

The SLR provides the theoretical and methodological basis for this research direction; it does not constitute an empirical validation of VCLF.
